How I Work

Approach

A three-step way of working, where every step carries a real project I actually ran — not a methodology template, but the order in which I genuinely work.

01

Read the signal: form the hypothesis first

READ THE SIGNAL

Work backwards from policy, industry cycles and competitor moves to the pressure the client is bound to run into, and set the hypothesis before the client has noticed it themselves. A visit without a hypothesis is just a chat — it never surfaces the real need.

Example: New disclosure rules from China's three major exchanges → clients' ESG rating needs (EY: an 80-page service proposal plus three rounds of client visits); everyone chasing AI short dramas → I judged that feature-length AI film demands far more on quality and spend per project, so I targeted studios doing high-quality short dramas and AI films (Tencent Cloud).
02

Validate in the field: pressure-test the hypothesis

TEST ON SITE

A hypothesis is not a requirement. Use repeated research and visits until the client admits this is their problem; at the same time convert qualitative gut feel into quantitative terms — otherwise you can neither quote nor prioritise.

Example: The Greater Bay Area tourism client took four rounds of research before we locked onto QA and live streaming; the AIGC client had quietly plugged in cheaper overseas models, so price comparison was a losing game → I found the one problem only we could solve: unified billing across models.
03

Translate it into something buyable, then push it to real consumption

TRANSLATE & CLOSE

Pin down technical requirements with architects and pre-sales, organise POCs and competitive benchmarks, choose the commercial path, then drive it all the way to actual consumption — and follow through on contract, settlement and invoicing.

Example: The AIGC film client completed Token Hub onboarding and moved into post-paid consumption; on the ByteDance Volcano Cloud migration, management accepted "optimise the existing estate first, discount second".

Profile

Everything above is what I have done. This section is who I am: where I started, the few decisions that mattered, and the tools in my hands.

I'm Ziyan Zhao. In the year I entered university I had the lowest provincial entrance score of that intake; my English couldn't keep up and I needed translation software just to follow lectures — that was my starting point. Four years later I graduated in Economics from the University of Nottingham Ningbo China: GPA 3.8/4.0, ranked 1st of 312 in my cohort for two consecutive years, UK First-Class Honours, outstanding graduate. Along the way I ranked first university-wide to win a full-tuition exchange at the University of Birmingham, and took about RMB 100K in undergraduate scholarships, becoming one of the faces of the university's marketing.

For my master's I read Management at the London School of Economics (LSE), with concurrent admits from LBS, NUS and Duke, and a postgraduate scholarship of RMB 80K. I chose on screening cost, not ranking: inside China, LBS has limited recognition outside top-tier finance and consulting, and with a Sino-foreign joint-venture undergraduate degree I needed a school name that required no extra explanation.

Professionally I've walked three paths: EY sustainability strategy consulting (Shanghai and Beijing offices, 9 months), JD Retail e-commerce operations and buying (Beijing HQ), and Tencent Cloud technical product business development trainee (Shenzhen HQ, current). All three share one thing — they are the same job: dig the real requirement out of messy information, then drive it into reality.

AI isn't a side interest, it's how I work. At JD I built automated workflows with JoyClaw AI and raised data extraction and analysis efficiency by over 60%. At Tencent Cloud I sell AI itself — working the full chain from reading the market to validating with data to finding the right person to uncovering the need, I independently sourced and closed an AIGC film studio, then drove it through Token Hub onboarding into post-paid consumption.
